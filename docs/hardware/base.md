# ASTRA - Base Construction

The ASTRA base is used to allow for the telescopes and electronics to be transported 
as a single unit. This allows easy movement of an assembled and working systems from 
an indoor location (e.g. a classroom) to an outdoor location where observations can be 
made (i.e. a field / tennis court / etc.). Generally it is necessary to disassemble the
major ASTRA components for transport by vehicle. 

The base is a key part of ASTRA as a 'science teaching cart' and the
wheels allow rolling the unit through normal doorways and over reasonably flat 
terrian (e.g. a parking lot or sidewalk). It is possible to use larger wheels and
construct an off-road variant but the overall size is cumbersome for indoor use. Care is
necessary to balance getting the unit through a normal door without disassemble and the
wheel clearance and tripod size. 

The base has wheels and leveling feet in addition to attachment points for the tripod and 
webbing for the enclosures. It is constructed using wood and off the shelf hardware using 
generally available tools. A 3D printed mounting bracket is used for holding the tripod feet 
and as a template for marking the needed hole locations and cut angles for the central 
connection point. 

The leveling feet in the design allow for physical leveling for ASTRA. They 
also serve to prevent ASTRA from moving when in use and this is important for consistent
motion of the antenna. The threaded rod used for these feet has moderately fine pitch threads.
This is somewhat inconvenient for initial leveling due to the number of turns needed. However,
it does allow for fine adjustment. Other types of feet (e.g. self leveling casters) can possibly
be used for areas which can accomodate lower clearance in the wheels. 

Note that precise assembly of the base is critical. In particular the alignment of the three
central members at their inner point needs to be done in a manner that ensures the mount base
members are planar. This leads to good alignment of the antenna and enables automatic pointing of the system. 

## Assembly Brackets

The ASTRA Base assembly brackets are 3D printed. They provide a template
for marking the base holes and cuts and are used on the base to hold
the tripod legs in place. 

## Parts

See the master bill of materials for details on purchase of items used in the base. 

A summary includes: 

- 1x 3 meter 38mm x 140mm / 10 ft 2x6 (high quality and smooth sufaced Fir or similar)
- 3x 3d Printed Mounting Brackets / Templates using PLA Plus 
- 24x Rubber Washers
- 3x Wheels
- 6x T-nuts
- 3x M12 200mm bolts
- 3x M12 leveling feet 
- 12x Metal washers
- 12x Metal nuts
- 12x Bolts
- 6 Pocket screws (2 inch self tapping, weather resistant)
- Wood glue
- Black spray paint
- Urethane clear coat spray paint
- MG Chemicals 841AR-340G Nickel EMI Paint

## Tools

A good tool kit is recommended. 

- Cutoff / circular / compound mitre saw
- Pocket screw jig and bit
- Electric drill
- 1/4” drill bit
- 1 7/8” drill bit
- Coarse and Fine Grit sand paper
- Hand sander

## Safety

- Eye protection when using power tools
- Gloves for handling the wood prior to sanding

## Procedure

1. Cut three 18 inch sections from the 2x6 using cross cuts to section the board.

2. Make a line 16 inches from the end of each section and place the triangular point of the template on that line.

3. Ensure that the template is square and aligned, then trace the triangular point of the template onto the boards. You will end up with three boards marked for cutting.

4. Using a fine tooth finish blade (e.g. 80T or higher). Cut each point with a mitre saw set to a 30° angle. Care should be taken to account for the saw kerf and to make each board identical to the others. 

5. Set the boards down and arrange to make all of the boards come to a single point, and have the outer edges line up well. If you have a problem it may be necessary to cut an additional section and replace a bad one. 

6. Label the faces that meet with the same numbers. Flip everything over and in a counterclockwise manner, draw arrows to indicate the direction that the pocket screws will be going. There are two pocket screws from each board into the next one. 

7. Clamp down the template onto the flat end of the board. Use a ¼ inch drill bit to drill the holes for the wheel bolts, and a 1&⅞ inch drill bit to drill the hole for the t-nuts. 

8. Using a pocket screw jig and bit, clamp the triangular board end down and drill the two holes for the pocket screws. 

9. Do a test fit of all of the hardware. This includes the template plates, washers, wheels, T-nuts, and threaded rods with feet and handles. Ensure good alignment and clean installation of all items.  

10. Remove the hardware for sanding and painting.

11. Sand the three sections using first a coarse grit (using a sander) and then a fine grit sandpaper (hand). Use the hand sander to round down the sharp edges. This is most needed at the square end of each piece. Do not sand the triangular faces, in order to make sure that they line up properly.

12. Clean the sections by vacuum and wiping to remove excess sawdust. 

13. Plug all of the holes except for the pocket screw holes with paper towels, then cover the triangular face with painters tape.

14. Setup an appropriate painting area where it is possible to hang the sections and paint them. 

15. Paint it black in 3-5 coats. Seal with 3-5 coats of clear coat. Let dry between coats for at a least an hour and only paint in temperatures above that recommended for the specific paint. 

16. Apply a thin layer of wood glue (e.g. Titebond III / Indoor and Outdoor glue). 

17. Using clamps and a protective layer (e.g. cardboard), sandwich two of the sections together. 

18. Hold the sections flat for installation of the pocket screws. They will want to move out and it is generally necessary to sandwich them. Care should be taken not to mess up the paint. 

19. Install the pocket screws (2” self tapping anti-corrosion) With the faces that will meet facing each other between two boards, such that the holes for the screws are still accessible, with glue in the joint. Put in the pocket screws.
Clean any wood glue the extrudes out with a damp cloth. 

20. Repeat installation of glue and pocket screws for the third board section. There will be two sets of screws to install. 

21. Reinstall the template plates, washers, wheels, T-nuts, threaded rods with feet, and handles. Loctite the threaded rod handles and wheel nuts. Ensure good alignment and clean installation of all items.  

22. Clean the completed base

23. Place the tripod on the base. Tripod should not have the central plate section. 

24. Use the Nite Ize MicroLock S-biners and the CamJam Tie down along with dacron cord (4 to 5mm). Cut three appropriate length sections of cord. Attach tripod legs to S-biners and CamJam cord, use the CamJam to tension the cord and hold the tripod down to the
base. The cord is looped through the base retaining loop and attached to the CamJam using two half-hitches. The S-biner is used to prevent wear from the cord on the tripod leg clip. The general configuration used is on the back of the CamJam package.
